# The Science Behind How Adults Learn New Languages

For decades, linguists believed that children have a natural advantage in language learning that adults simply can't overcome. Recent neuroscience is challenging that idea — and the findings are genuinely exciting for adult learners.

## The Critical Period Hypothesis: More Nuanced Than You Think

The "critical period" for language acquisition — typically associated with early childhood — is real for achieving native-like pronunciation. But for grammar, vocabulary, and communicative fluency? Adults actually have several advantages over children.

Adults bring enormous existing knowledge of how language, context, and meaning work. A child learns "dog" by repeated exposure; an adult can simply be told the equivalent in the target language and immediately understand it relationally.

## Spaced Repetition: The Most Evidence-Backed Learning Method

Of all the study techniques research has examined, spaced repetition stands out with remarkable consistency. The principle: review information at increasing intervals just as you're about to forget it.

Your brain consolidates memories during sleep. Studying before sleep, then reviewing the next morning, then in three days, then in a week — this dramatically outperforms daily cramming of the same material.

## Comprehensible Input: Stephen Krashen's Enduring Legacy

Linguist Stephen Krashen proposed that language acquisition happens primarily through exposure to "comprehensible input" — material that's slightly above your current level (what he called "i+1").

This means the most effective practice isn't studying grammar tables. It's consuming language content you can almost — but not quite — fully understand. Films with subtitles in the target language, graded readers, and conversations with patient tutors all provide this.

## The Role of Emotional Engagement

Emotionally charged experiences are remembered better — this is well established in memory research. Language learned in emotionally engaging contexts (real conversations, travel situations, meaningful relationships) sticks far more effectively than language learned from textbook exercises.

This is one reason working with a real human tutor consistently outperforms app-based study for long-term retention: the emotional engagement is higher, and the stakes feel real.

## What This Means for Your Practice

The research points to a clear optimal approach:
1. Get maximum comprehensible input through listening and reading
2. Use spaced repetition for vocabulary
3. Practice speaking in high-engagement, real contexts
4. Sleep consistently — your brain does critical language processing overnight
